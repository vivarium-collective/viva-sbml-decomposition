Arm 9 — Agent-based model vs its mean-field SBML reduction (the adversarial arm)

The whole investigation assumes the model you start with is an SBML/ODE network. But can an AGENT-BASED model — a population of discrete individuals with stochastic interactions — even BE an SBML/Tellurium model? If you reduce it to a mean-field ODE and run it in libroadrunner/CVODE, where does that reduction hold and where does it structurally break?

Claim. An agent-based model reduces to an SBML/Tellurium mean-field ODE ONLY in the large-population, well-mixed, deterministic limit; it structurally cannot capture stochasticity, discreteness, extinction, or individual heterogeneity. MEASURED (Lotka-Volterra, base rates r=a=b=m=1, start density (1.8, 1.0), horizon ~2 periods): at a LARGE population scale K=400 (80 SSA runs) the mean-field ODE reproduces the SSA ensemble mean to a max relative error of 4.9% with 0% of runs going extinct — the reduction is VALID. At a SMALL scale K=8 (200 SSA runs) the ensemble mean diverges from the ODE by 68% max relative error and 94% of runs go EXTINCT before the horizon, hitting an absorbing state (prey or predator count 0) that the neutrally-stable ODE — which simply orbits forever — can never reach. The ODE additionally cannot represent the ensemble variance at ANY K. So "can an ABM be an SBML model?" has an honest, quantified answer: yes, but only as its large-population mean-field limit, and that reduction silently discards exactly the stochastic/discrete phenomena (extinction, demographic noise) that are often the biological point. This breaks the naive "just write it as SBML" claim.

Where arm 9 sits on the 3-way tradeoff

Findings

  • REDUCTION VALID AT LARGE N — at population scale K=400 (80 Gillespie-SSA runs, ~2 Lotka-Volterra periods) the mean-field ODE integrated by libroadrunner/CVODE reproduces the SSA ensemble mean to a max relative error of 4.9% (mean 1.0%), and 0% of runs go extinct. This is the Kurtz large-population limit in action: at enough individuals the discrete stochastic model and its continuous deterministic SBML reduction agree, so the ABM genuinely DOES reduce to an SBML model here.
  • REDUCTION INVALID AT SMALL N — at scale K=8 (200 SSA runs) 94% of runs go EXTINCT before the horizon (prey or predator count hits 0, an absorbing state), while the mean-field ODE — neutrally stable closed orbits — simply cycles forever and NEVER goes extinct. The SSA ensemble mean diverges from the ODE by 68% max relative error. The deterministic SBML reduction misses the single most important qualitative behaviour of the small-population system (that it dies out).
  • STRUCTURAL LIMIT, NOT A TOLERANCE — the gap is not something a finer integrator or looser tolerance closes. The ODE state is (prey, predator) concentrations; it has no representation of integer discreteness, of demographic noise variance, or of the extinction probability, at ANY K. Those are properties of the ensemble and the absorbing boundary, which a single deterministic trajectory cannot carry. The ABM was NOT tuned to match the ODE — the large-N agreement and the small-N divergence are both measured.
  • FEASIBILITY STATEMENT — "can an ABM even be an SBML/Tellurium model?" Answer: yes, but ONLY as its large-population, well-mixed, deterministic mean-field limit. That reduction is a real, runnable SBML model (the predator_prey_meanfield composite's Antimony) and is faithful when individuals are many; it silently discards stochasticity, discreteness, extinction and individual heterogeneity, which at small population size ARE the behaviour. This is the adversarial result that breaks the naive "just decompose/rewrite any model as SBML" claim the rest of the investigation operates under.

Model — the mean-field SBML reduction (Lotka-Volterra, Antimony)

This is the mean-field ODE reduction (the predator_prey_meanfield composite), integrated by libroadrunner/CVODE — a real, runnable SBML model. The agent-based model itself (predator_prey_abm, a Gillespie-SSA process over integer populations) is NOT an SBML model: it has no continuous RHS, and its stochasticity / discreteness / extinction cannot be written as ODE state. That gap is the finding.

Show the mean-field Lotka-Volterra Antimony
model lotka_volterra_meanfield
  # Mean-field (large-population, deterministic) limit of the stochastic ABM.
  # Species are continuous population counts; K = 400 is the system size.
  Prey = 720;
  Pred = 400;
  r = 1.0;   # prey birth (per capita)
  a = 1.0;   # predation
  b = 1.0;   # predator birth from predation
  m = 1.0;   # predator death (per capita)
  K = 400;

  prey_birth:     => Prey; r*Prey;
  predation:      Prey => ; (a/K)*Prey*Pred;
  predator_birth: => Pred; (b/K)*Prey*Pred;
  predator_death: Pred => ; m*Pred;
end
