SBML in Tellurium vs process-bigraph decomposition — a speed × accuracy × composability tradeoff

A methods comparison, not a biology result. One curated SBML metabolic model (Teusink glycolysis, BioModels BIOMD0000000064, 17 floating species) is run along a spectrum of implementations under one workspace, each measured on the same three axes. The deliverable is a decision guide: which decomposition patterns to use, which to avoid, and why.

Verdict (passing-with-caveats): Decomposition is never free on speed or accuracy, but the monolith cannot be composed. Monolithic Tellurium is the fastest and the numerical reference; every decomposition adds orchestration overhead and operator-splitting error that shrinks as the coupling interval shrinks. The payoff is strictly on the composability axis: only the decomposed arms can substitute a submodel, couple through a shared store, or mix engines (arm 6 runs half libroadrunner / half native in one composite — a thing a single SBML model structurally cannot do). The ensemble arm is the one place vivarium wins on wall-clock (7.5× via Ray on 16 cores), because libroadrunner does not release the GIL so only OS-process parallelism helps.

Scientific argument

Main claim. On the implementation spectrum for an SBML model, speed and accuracy are best at the monolithic pole and composability is best at the decomposed pole; the choice is a tradeoff with no dominating point, and the quantified costs make the decision rule explicit.

Evidence for

  • Monolithic Tellurium (arm 1) is the accuracy reference and matches direct libroadrunner to ~1e-6 on a shared time grid.
  • Operator-split (arm 2) and modular (arm 3) both show real operator-splitting error that shrinks monotonically with the coupling interval (arm 2: 0.587→0.067; arm 3: 0.468→0.100), confirming the error is controllable but non-zero.
  • Fully-native process-bigraph (arm 4) reproduces the reference to ~1e-7 with a hand-ported RHS — the far pole is achievable at high fidelity but hand-written.
  • Arm 6 runs a cross-engine hybrid (upper glycolysis in libroadrunner, lower glycolysis native) sharing one store — a composition a monolith cannot express — and reproduces arm 3 to 3.2e-7.
  • The ensemble arm (arm 5) achieves a real 7.5× speedup via Ray OS processes on 16 cores.

Evidence against / nuance

  • overhead_fraction (framework share of wall-clock) is HIGHEST for the monolith (~0.32) and lowest for the decomposed/native arms (~0.02-0.05) — a cheap process makes the framework look expensive, so 'framework share' is NOT the decomposition cost. The decomposition cost is the wall-clock ratio to monolithic plus absolute framework seconds.
  • At 17 species the scalar-dict vs numpy-array leaf choice is perf-irrelevant (arm 4 variants indistinguishable); the scalar-dict penalty is linear in element count and only bites at 100s-1000s of species.

Caveats

  • Single model (Teusink glycolysis) so far; generalization to a stiff oscillator and a modular signaling cascade is the next increment.
  • A clean operator split (arm 2) was only possible across the weakly-coupled glycogen/trehalose sinks; splitting across the strong P/ATP/ADP energy coupling diverges — do not generalize arm 2's splitting error to arbitrary decompositions.
  • Mismatched-interval decompositions carry an O(P^2) multi-rate scheduler cost in pb ≤1.10.x; process-bigraph PR #270 (unmerged as of 2026-10-25) reduces it to ~O(P log P). Our arms use equal intervals, the cheap regime. Prefer equal coupling intervals today.

Decision guide

Biological story

The vehicle is yeast glycolysis (Teusink et al. 2000), a 17-species kinetic model with a stiff phosphate/energy coupling and a square-root adenylate assignment rule. It is not studied here for its biology — it is a well-characterized, moderately stiff reaction network that exercises the numerical and structural stressors a decomposition has to survive.

Read the arms as a spectrum. Arm 1 hands the whole SBML model to libroadrunner/CVODE in one piece — fast, exact, opaque. Arm 2 keeps the same model but advances it in operator-split sub-steps that exchange state through a vivarium store each coupling interval; this is a mechanical split, and it reveals that splitting across the strong energy coupling diverges while a weak-seam split converges. Arm 3 cuts the model along a genuine biological seam (upper vs lower glycolysis at the aldolase/F16P boundary) into two coupled process nodes that reconcile additively through the real process-bigraph apply path — the first point where the model is genuinely composable. Arm 4 discards libroadrunner entirely and integrates a hand-written native RHS, the far pole of composability. Arm 5 fans the monolith out into a parameter ensemble on a real parallel backend. Arm 6 is the punchline: a single composite running half in libroadrunner and half in native Python, sharing one species store — the capability a monolith can never offer.
