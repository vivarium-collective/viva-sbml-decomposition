SBML in Tellurium vs process-bigraph decomposition — speed × accuracy × composability, and the limits of SBML itself

A methods investigation, not a biology result. Seven studies run SBML models — and, at the edges, a scaled multi-organ body and a stochastic agent-based model — across a spectrum of implementations (on a small set of generic composites) under one workspace, each measured on speed, accuracy, and composability. The deliverable is a decision guide: which decomposition patterns to use, which to avoid, where Tellurium strains, and where SBML stops applying.

Verdict (passing-with-caveats): No single implementation wins on all axes. Monolithic Tellurium is the fastest implementation and the numerical reference; every decomposition adds orchestration overhead and additive-split (Jacobi) coupling error that shrinks as the coupling interval shrinks. The payoff is strictly composability: only the decomposed studies can substitute a submodel, couple through a store, or mix engines (cross-engine-coupling runs half libroadrunner / half a second engine in one composite — impossible for a monolith — and the engines are interchangeable at the seam). The ensemble study is the one place vivarium wins on wall-clock (7.5× via Ray). Two boundaries sharpen the picture: as a model scales, the monolithic libroadrunner BUILD time strains superlinearly (~O(N^1.6)) — but decomposition is not a free win (it is slower end-to-end at every size we measured, paying off only at very large models, and with accuracy degradation); and an ABM reduces to an SBML/Tellurium ODE only in the large-population limit — at small populations the ODE misses the stochastic collapse entirely (predators go extinct in 86.5% of runs; the ODE cycles forever).

Scientific argument

Main claim. On the implementation spectrum, speed and accuracy are best at the monolithic pole and composability at the decomposed pole — a genuine tradeoff with no dominating point — and the tool itself has limits: Tellurium's monolithic build strains superlinearly at scale, and SBML cannot represent an agent-based model outside its mean-field limit.

Evidence for

  • sbml-in-tellurium (monolithic) matches direct libroadrunner to ~1e-6 and is the fastest implementation; bulk-port binding makes it ~12% faster, bit-identical.
  • mechanical-split-cost and biological-modular-seam show real split error that shrinks with the coupling interval (0.587->0.067; 0.468->0.100). mechanical-split-cost now uses additive (Jacobi) coupling via the generic reaction_split composite rather than the earlier sequential Lie split.
  • fully-native-pbg (fully native) reaches ~1e-7 fidelity with no libroadrunner at runtime; at 17 species the scalar-dict vs numpy-array leaf choice is perf-irrelevant.
  • cross-engine-coupling runs a cross-engine hybrid (libroadrunner + a second engine in one composite) that a monolith cannot express, reproducing biological-modular-seam to 3.2e-7 with the native lower half — the composability payoff; engines are interchangeable at the seam.
  • ensemble-and-multirate (same-rate ensemble) achieves a real 7.5x wall-clock speedup via Ray.
  • ensemble-and-multirate (multi-rate ensemble) shows process-bigraph #270's heap scheduler is bit-identical to the scan loop and its multi-rate advantage grows with process count (1.0x->1.64x over P=4->256).
  • bigger-models-and-abm-limit measures the monolithic libroadrunner BUILD straining superlinearly (~O(N^1.64), 0.20s->18.5s over 97->1537 species).
  • bigger-models-and-abm-limit shows the ABM->SBML mean-field reduction is valid at large population (K=400: 4.9% error, no extinction).

Evidence against / nuance

  • overhead_fraction (framework SHARE of wall-clock) is HIGHEST for the cheap monolith (~0.32) and lowest for decomposed/native studies — a cheap process makes the framework look expensive, so share is NOT the decomposition cost.
  • Decomposition is slower END-TO-END than the monolith at every multi-organ size measured (bigger-models-and-abm-limit); it only build-wins at ~769+ species, and its accuracy degrades at scale (0.31 at 385 species).
  • A clean mechanical split (mechanical-split-cost) was only possible across a weakly-coupled seam; splitting across the strong energy coupling diverges.
  • At small population the ABM→SBML reduction is INVALID (bigger-models-and-abm-limit, K=8): the ODE misses the stochastic collapse the ABM shows (predator extinction 86.5%, total 51%).

Caveats

  • Core comparison is one metabolic model (Teusink glycolysis); bigger-models-and-abm-limit holds the boundary probes with their own synthetic/biological vehicles.
  • Mismatched coupling intervals carry an O(P²) scheduler cost in pb ≤1.10.x; process-bigraph #270 (merged) reduces it to ~O(P log P). Prefer equal intervals on older pins.
  • bigger-models-and-abm-limit: the decomposed multi-organ run at very large N (≥256 species) was left unmeasured; no end-to-end scale win is claimed there.
  • bigger-models-and-abm-limit: ABM extinction fractions are parameter- and scale-specific; the qualitative limit (ODE shows zero extinction) is general.
